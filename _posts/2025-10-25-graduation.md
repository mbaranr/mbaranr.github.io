As the winner of this year's Chancellor's Medal, I had to deliver a speech to LUL's faculty members, my fellow graduates, friends and family. For those who missed it, here is the transcript:

---

<em>Academic Dean, Pro Vice Chancellor, Campus Director, faculty members, family, friends, and of course, my fellow graduates. I am truly honoured and grateful for this incredible recognition.</em>

<em>Most of you have probably seen me around campus before, but for those who haven’t: hi, I’m Matias, born and raised in Lima, Peru.</em>

<em>Peru is a beautiful place: rich in history, home to one of the seven wonders of the world, and some of the best food you’ll ever taste. But when it comes to technology… let’s just say we’re still catching up. The closest I got to programming in high school was making Excel spreadsheets. That’s all you need to know.</em>

<em>Still, I had a vision. And with no experience, I decided to pursue a computer science degree halfway across the Atlantic.</em>

<em>I was 17 when I arrived in Leipzig. I was clueless, very lost. And if it weren’t for the warmth and generosity of some of the people sitting here today, I honestly don’t know where I’d be.</em>

<em>I’m sure many of you can relate. You know what it’s like to leave home for the first time. And I want to emphasize that the courage it takes to make that decision, to take that risk, deserves recognition in itself.</em>

<em>And on the topic of risk, there’s a phrase a dear friend of mine once said that has stuck with me ever since: “There’s no freedom without risk.”</em>

<em>When I agreed to give some words today, I risked making a fool of myself.  
When choosing the members for your group projects, you risked having to do most of the work.  
When you love someone, you risk not being loved in return.  
When applying for a job, you risk rejection.  
But those who risk nothing, ultimately do nothing.</em>

<em>Jonathan Haidt, a great social psychologist, published a book last year titled <em>The Anxious Generation</em>. In it, he argues that Gen Z (people like us) are, among many things, mediocre risk-takers.</em>

<em>So I stand here today and ask you: let’s try to prove him wrong.</em>

<em>But I do understand that with risk failing is sometimes inevitable. I go as far to say that if you don’t fail, you are not even trying.</em>

<em>Mario Vargas Llosa, Peru’s only Nobel Laureate, once said: “Good literature is not a product of happiness."</em>

<em>So if you’re looking to write a memorable story for your life, expect to fall face first here and there.</em>

<em>What’s reassuring, though, is that I know that after every fall, I’ve found a community that’s there to lift me up. Because we’ve learned as much about community here as we’ve learned about Computer Science, Software Development, Business, Accounting, and so on.</em>

<em>So as we step into whatever comes next, let’s keep taking risks. Let’s fail with dignity. And let’s keep writing stories worth remembering.</em>

<em>Mom, Dad, I can’t express in words how grateful I am for your continued support.</em>

<em>Congratulations Class of 2025, we made it.</em>
